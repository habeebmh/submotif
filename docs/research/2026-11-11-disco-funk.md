# Disco and Funk: research for the Undertow generator revision

Research date: 11 November 2026. This is a design brief, not a claim that the proposed revision has been implemented or listening-tested. Sources below were read as original lessons, an artist lesson transcript, or original research. No published riffs or MIDI patterns are proposed for copying into a pattern bank.

The practical correction is to construct a recognizable bass phrase, then develop it. Choosing individually legal scale notes does not establish a harmonic foundation, and independently randomizing every onset, octave, and duration undermines an identifiable idea. Generate can construct a new motif on every click while preserving that motif within the resulting phrase.

There are deliberate counterexamples to a minimal repeating-riff approach. In his [original artist lesson on Vulfpeck's “Dean Town”](https://www.guitarworld.com/lessons/artist-lessons/cory-wong-doubling-basslines-on-guitar), Cory Wong describes a long, through-composed bass melody. The proposed conservative defaults are a response to this product's overly random output, not a definition or limit of funk musicianship.

## Proposed generation procedure

Everything in this section is an **implementation heuristic inferred from the sources**, not a published statistical model. Numbers are starting points for listening comparisons and must not be described as measured genre norms.

1. **Establish the harmonic assumption.** Without incoming chords or a chord progression, treat the phrase as a tonic vamp. Select a tonic triad from the chosen scale. Do not invent chord changes. Distinguish a phrase's structural notes from temporary approach notes before choosing pitches.
2. **Construct one new core motif.** Draw an onset structure, accent roles, and a short pitch contour together, using a fresh seed. For four bars, derive a related response and ending from this motif while returning to the original idea. For two bars, retain the first bar's identity in the second. A one-bar loop needs no forced extra fill. These are procedural transformations of newly made material, never selection from saved riffs.
3. **Choose structural pitches first.** At the normal Movement setting, start with root/fifth emphasis and occasional scale-compatible thirds. As an initial conservative target, let at least 75% of new attacks belong to the tonic triad. Bias important or long notes toward the root/fifth. This percentage is a design proposal, not research data, and includes no requirement to erase intentionally locked color notes.
4. **Connect to known destinations.** Reserve optional short pickups, then choose an adjacent allowed scale pitch that resolves into a known target. Start with no more than one such connector per bar at default Movement. A connector must have a destination, including across the loop boundary; do not end a phrase on an accidental unresolved color because the next note was chosen independently. When the allowed scale has no suitably close connecting pitch, omit the connector.
5. **Constrain variation to a role.** Retain at least three quarters of the core onset/pitch-role pairs in an ordinary response; change a small ending, omit a note, or repeat a target. Place larger fills near phrase boundaries. This retention threshold is a reviewable implementation target, not a musical law. Avoid mandatory fills on every bar.
6. **Compose articulation with the motif.** Reuse intentional strong/weak roles in repeats; add only small bounded velocity differences after those roles exist. Preserve meaningful gaps. Gate should modify an articulated rhythm rather than erase all rests. Generate note-off before the next monophonic note unless legato is an explicit feature.

## Separate Disco and Funk behavior

| Decision | Disco profile proposal | Funk profile proposal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rhythmic identity | Build an eighth-note foundation with a few linked sixteenth attacks. Reuse a low/high gesture at equivalent positions. | Build a compact syncopated cell with a clear point of return and deliberate gaps; answer it without replacing its skeleton. |
| Octaves | With Octave Jumps enabled, choose the octave relationship for the gesture, then repeat that relationship. A repeated upper attack can be a density addition. | Use an upper-register answer as one planned event, with a return to the low register. Do not scatter independent high notes through the whole motif. |
| Pitch role | Root and fifth support the repeating groove; occasional correct thirds or short resolving approaches provide motion. | Structural chord tones support a recognizable rhythmic idea. A small scale-compatible answer or phrase-ending approach provides contrast. |
| Density | Add/remove related repeated attacks before expanding the pitch vocabulary. | Add/remove response notes while retaining core accents and some empty subdivisions. A high setting may be busy but should still have phrase identity. |
| Articulation | Short enough to distinguish repeated attacks, with consistent paired-note accents. | Short supporting attacks plus intentional longer targets or gaps. Greater rhythmic detail must not automatically mean more pitch classes. |
| Avoid | Continuous unrelated scale runs masquerading as disco; an octave coin flip on each step. | Randomly filling every hole; treating all 16 subdivisions as unrelated opportunities; claiming soft notes are physical ghost notes. |

These profiles are intentionally narrower than the genres. In particular, a generic MIDI-only generator cannot reproduce plucking position, string muting, slap/pop, hammer-ons, slides, or a live bassist's interaction with a drummer merely by changing MIDI velocity.

## Scale, register, and existing-control constraints

- Find pitch roles by semitone interval, not array index. All five current scales contain a tonic minor or major triad: use offsets **0, 3, 7** in Minor, Dorian, Phrygian, and Minor Pentatonic; **0, 4, 7** in Major. Minor Pentatonic's third array element is the fourth degree, not the chord's third.
- The common dominant-seventh lesson vocabulary needs a major third and flat seventh. Undertow's Major scale has a major seventh, while the minor-family scales have a minor third. Do not silently add a flat seventh to Major or a major third to Minor. Supporting Mixolydian or explicit chord qualities is a separate feature, not a hidden correction.
- Phrygian's flat second is a distinctive color. Under this conservative tonic-vamp design, treat it as an optional short resolving neighbor, not a frequent long structural bass note. This restriction is a product choice, not a claim that Phrygian compositions forbid sustained flat seconds.
- Keep adjacent non-octave structural notes in a coherent register. Choose an upper phrase destination before moving there. Pitch proximity can help continuity, but octave figures intentionally violate a smallest-leap preference; do not smooth away the Disco profile's defining gesture.
- Root Only restricts pitch class to the tonic. With Octave Jumps disabled it must not create new octave displacement; with it enabled, root octaves can still form a phrase. Neither option should replace musical rhythm with uniform repeated hits.
- Root Anchor governs the first sounded new note according to the established product contract. It must not move a delayed onset to beat one. Locked notes retain priority over a newly chosen scale, root, role, or motif; do not “repair” them silently. Rebuild unlocked material around their timing and pitch where possible.
- Reducing Bars can crop locks outside the loop as already documented. Inside the loop, role constraints are subordinate to exact lock preservation.

## Checks that can support, but cannot replace, listening

Use fixed seeds to compare the existing and revised engines. Record motif retention across bars; strong-position chord-tone share; whether each deliberately generated connector reaches its target; unnecessary non-octave register jumps; repeated-role velocity consistency; and actual time left silent. Test every scale/root-toggle combination and conflicting locked notes. Avoid conflating high diversity with good music.

Then audition fresh seeds using the same bass sound, tempo, and drum accompaniment, both alone and against the stated tonic harmony. Include low, default, and high Density/Movement, as well as octave-disabled examples. Assess whether the listener can recognize the recurring idea, whether endings lead back convincingly, and whether the bass supports the harmony. Differences between sound presets must be distinguished from composition changes. Structural tests do not establish that a groove sounds good; no such listening claim is made by this document.
