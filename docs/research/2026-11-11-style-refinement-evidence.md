# Style refinement: relationships inside a newly generated bass motif

Research date: 11 November 2026. Historical baseline: code diagnoses below describe repository HEAD `595c60e`, before the subsequent refinement, not the current source. This note supplements [house/acid research](2026-11-11-house-acid.md) and [Disco/Funk research](2026-11-11-disco-funk.md), and preserves source reading and implementation proposals; see [MUSIC_THEORY](../MUSIC_THEORY.md) for implemented behavior. It is not an implementation or listening-test completion record. No commercial MIDI, song transcription, or complete tutorial riff has been collected as a generation corpus.

## What still needs correction

The existing engine already repeats motifs, assigns mostly triadic pitches, and uses resolving connectors. Recommending those features again does not address the user's criticism. Reading `plugin/src/PatternEngine.cpp` before this refinement identified narrower issues:

- `makeRhythm` fixes steps 0 and 8 for Deep, Acid, Disco, and Funk. Rolling fixes 0 and 6, then copies the two-beat half. Outside Offbeat, a delayed first attack is unavailable even when Start on root is disabled. Those are product restrictions, not requirements established by the research.
- Most optional onsets are weighted samples of individual grid positions. A high weight on a sixteenth before a beat does not ensure that the following beat is left empty. It can produce an added pickup instead of an anticipated structural attack.
- `articulate` usually gives the highest velocity to beats one and three, with odd sixteenths quieter. An intentionally displaced main attack therefore tends to lose emphasis. Rolling uses one short duration family before spacing limits, despite the earlier research's suggestion of contrasting pulses and longer anchors.
- Deep and Acid largely share a pitch rule: move from tonic to one selected triadic color after a half-bar pivot. Funk assigns color to a particular grid class. These are positional assignments rather than contours constructed around actual phrase destinations.
- `addConnectors` runs after rhythm, pitch, and articulation. It can add an attack to an intended gap or replace a chosen repeat. Scale legality and eventual resolution do not prove that this addition preserves the hook. A connector can also make Melody change attack density independently of Density.

These observations are code-level diagnoses, not listening findings. They suggest improving the relationships among events and silences before adding more pitch vocabulary or more multi-bar forms.

## A directly relevant experiment, and its boundary

Sean-Lee Duncan and Guido Orgs (2024), [Move Your Body! Low-frequency Amplitude and Syncopation Increase Groove Perception in House Music](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10204599/1/Move%20your%20Body.pdf), *Music Perception* 42(2), 95–108, [DOI 10.1525/MP.2024.42.2.95](https://doi.org/10.1525/MP.2024.42.2.95), tested 179 participants with 20 house clips. Methods, results, discussion, and limitations were inspected. Bass rhythm was manipulated against a constant quarter-note kick: higher syncopation placed attacks on weaker positions and rests on stronger positions; the lower condition shifted attacks toward stronger positions. The combination of greater bass low-frequency energy and higher syncopation received the strongest urge-to-move ratings.

This is closer to the product than a drum-break experiment, but it tested only two syncopation levels, used drums/bass/piano together, and did not measure aesthetic preference. The authors explicitly say their design cannot provide a predictive groove model. It supplies neither six genre profiles nor generation probabilities. It supports auditioning displaced bass emphasis against a stable kick; it does not prove that maximum syncopation, more notes, or louder playback improves generated composition.

## Six concrete style corrections

All recommendations below are authored implementation heuristics inferred from the sources. They are candidates to compare by listening, not empirical genre laws. Use the existing tonic-vamp assumption until chord information exists.

| Profile | Rhythm relationship to generate | Pitch and articulation relationship | Correction to prioritize |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Deep House** | Generate a main arrival plus a measured answer, with a protected gap. Allow an arrival to fall before or after a metric boundary and leave the boundary unstruck when it is anticipated. | Keep a low recurring support pitch; let the answer leave and return to a chosen destination. A sustained arrival may be syncopated; it need not always occur at step 0 or 8. Shorten the answer relative to the main arrival. | Remove permanent two-per-bar downbeat anchors as the only possible skeleton. Preserve space in actual sounding time. S8/S9 support the dimensions; the exact Deep weighting has weak direct source support. |
| **Rolling / tech-house** | Sample a small group of related attacks, a release gap, and a recurring response. Repeated pulses can follow an anchor or lead into it. Allow either shared kick accents or a delayed cell; do not turn the adjacent techno/trance technique of filling every non-kick sixteenth into the house default. | Repeated pitch supplies propulsion. Use contrasting lengths for a held target and detached repeats; do not force a pitch change at the last event of every two-beat half. Repeat the chosen role pattern. | Replace the single short gate family and immutable 0/6 skeleton. Retain useful half-cell recurrence without requiring perfect symmetry in every generation. S1 directly supports the articulation correction; exact kick-spacing weights remain a design choice. |
| **Offbeat** | Keep main eighths between quarter-note kicks. At low density, omit an event as a recurring omission; at higher density, attach a pickup to an existing pulse rather than sampling arbitrary odd steps. | Main pulses share a recognizable duration family with release before or at the next assumed kick. A pickup is quieter/shorter, with the same repeated pitch or a purposeful resolution. Pitch answers need not always occupy the final offbeat. | Keep the existing backbone, but condition extras on their parent pulse and preserve a consistent end-of-note relationship. S7 supports the backbone and release target; the omission/attachment policy is our extension. |
| **Acid** | Construct recurring groups of gated events separated by intentional rests. Sample accent destinations and sustained connections separately from the pitch plan. An accent can anticipate a kick without forcing every metric downbeat louder. | Keep a compact recurring contour. Accent and length are separate roles: allow short accented notes and longer unaccented ones. A deliberate leap belongs to the contour, not to all accented positions. | Decouple accent from long duration and metric anchor hierarchy. Protect the articulation sequence when Melody changes. S6/S9 support separation of dimensions; ordinary MIDI still cannot guarantee true 303 slides. |
| **Disco** | Keep the eighth-pulse identity but let density split selected response events into related repeated attacks. Some pulse omissions can frame a turnaround; do not fill every odd subdivision indiscriminately. | When octave jumps are enabled, couple low/high events into recurring gestures. The upper member may be shorter and optionally doubled. Root/fifth or an existing allowed pitch can serve the same rhythmic role when octave jumps are off. | Attach doubling to an actual response and preserve that gesture's register and release contour. S4/S5 directly support this difference from generic eighth-note sampling. |
| **Funk** | Sample a recognizable accented foundation, then anticipate a selected arrival and suppress its original attack. Attach a short repeat or lead-in to a foundation event. Keep some support coincident with the implied beat. | A displaced structural tone can be the strongest note. Its following short answer can return to the root or another selected stable target. Avoid assigning the same color to every occurrence of one grid class. | Separate structural accent from metrical strength; use density for optional attached events while retaining silent responses. S2/S3 directly support anticipation and foundation-preserving decoration. Ghost-note realism remains instrument-dependent. |

## Fresh weighted generation without a bassline bank

The proposal is a conditional event generator, not selection among named or stored rhythms. Do not implement these tables as a list of complete masks. On every Generate, freshly sample the number and placement of structural events, their relationships, optional decorations, contour, and articulation under style weights and current controls.

An implementable construction sequence is:

1. **Choose relationships before pitches.** Sample a main arrival, response window, and silence window. Subsequent event probabilities depend on distance to already chosen events and the implied beat. A repeat needs a parent; an anticipation needs a displaced destination. The positions and combinations remain newly generated.
2. **Make silence operative.** A protected rest constrains both attacks and note tails. Optional decoration and connector passes must respect it. Where a long note is intentional, identify the following empty steps as sustain rather than silence.
3. **Assign repeated pitch roles to actual events.** Select a small vocabulary and a return target, then construct departure/repetition/return around the sampled rhythm. Do not map color permanently to all `step % 4 == 3` positions or the latter part of every half-bar. Preserve all selected-scale and Root notes only constraints.
4. **Give roles their accents and releases.** A syncopated arrival may be loud; a metrically strong supporting pulse may be quiet. Sample a recurring length relationship, then scale it with Note length subject to the next onset, intended silence, loop endpoint, and any profile-specific kick boundary.
5. **Decorate within the attack budget.** Adding an approach or repeated upper note consumes an optional event slot. A displaced arrival replaces its original event. Density should control this budget; Melody should primarily control pitch-role detail. Existing connector checks remain useful after role-aware selection.
6. **Repeat and transform the fresh result.** Retain the current phrase-level repetition mechanism where it works. A response can alter one sampled relationship, including silence or articulation, instead of always changing a final pitch. Exact locks take precedence throughout.

A quarter-note kick grid is only an explicit audition/composition assumption, because the device does not receive the user's drum part. Never describe the generated line as following an analyzed kick. Nor should all styles receive a blanket rule prohibiting coincident kick/bass attacks. MIDI note-off does not stop an instrument's release tail; note separation is not a promise of acoustic separation.

## Artist references and evidence strength

Mark Knight, Mau P, Chris Lake, and HUGEL remain taste references, not statistically characterized models. Their direct process interviews are already documented in the earlier house/acid note. They support hook coherence and working with the surrounding track; they do not provide evidence for numerical onset, pitch, or gate distributions. The current pass found usable original producer/educator lessons, but no fully examined lesson by those four artists establishing the six exact profiles. Chris Lake's DJ Mag session remains an untranscribed video reference, so it is not evidence here for specific notes or timings.

The strongest new implementation support is for anticipation as a relationship, attached repeats, length contrast, and independent accents. The weakest support is for any precise Deep House versus modern tech-house probability split, any universal HUGEL/Latin bass formula, or a numerical authenticity score. Do not invent those.

## Focused comparison to perform after implementation

Hold seed, patch, tempo, and accompaniment constant while comparing the old and proposed construction. Test root-only cases first: a convincing rhythm should survive without melodic novelty. Then compare ordinary melodic settings and the allowed octave option. Include multiple fresh seeds per style and low/default/high density; do not choose only the best example.

Useful diagnostics now include displaced-arrival pairs with their original attack absent; emphasis retained on structural offbeats; actual silent duration rather than empty-grid count; repeated pitch/gate/accent relationships inside a bar; decorations attached to parents; and whether connector insertion defeats a protected rest or density budget. These diagnose the proposed changes. None certifies groove or substitutes for the user's audition in a track.
